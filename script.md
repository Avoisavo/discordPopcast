# School Taught Us Everything Except the Actual Lesson

**Emy:** Be honest—what is the most useful thing school taught you that was never written in a textbook?

**Liam:** How to look busy when you have absolutely no idea what is happening.

**Emy:** That explains a lot.

**Liam:** It was a skill. You hold a pen, look at the board, and nod every few seconds.

**Emy:** The nod has to be believable.

**Liam:** Exactly. Too fast and you look confused. Too slow and you look like you're falling asleep.

**Emy:** Mine was controlling a noisy room without saying anything.

**Liam:** You mean glaring at people?

**Emy:** Strategic eye contact.

**Liam:** Intimidation.

**Emy:** Classroom management. You look directly at the loudest person for two seconds, then wait.

**Liam:** That only works if they respect you.

**Emy:** No. It works if they think you're about to say their full name.

**Liam:** Okay. That's terrifying.

**Emy:** And effective.

**Liam:** Did you actually use this recently?

**Emy:** Yesterday. Everyone in the studio was talking at once, so I looked at one person and the whole room went quiet.

**Liam:** Maybe they thought you were angry.

**Emy:** I wasn't angry.

**Liam:** You have a very serious resting face.

**Emy:** I have an attentive resting face.

**Liam:** You looked like you were about to return someone's exam paper face-down.

**Emy:** The important thing is that it worked.

**Liam:** Fair. School accidentally taught me something useful too.

**Emy:** How to copy homework without matching the other person's mistakes?

**Liam:** I never copied homework.

**Emy:** You copied homework.

**Liam:** I participated in collaborative learning.

**Emy:** Of course you did.

**Liam:** My real lesson came during a History presentation. The projector died halfway through.

**Emy:** Completely?

**Liam:** Completely. Black screen. Nothing.

**Emy:** What did the teacher do?

**Liam:** Pressed every button on the remote.

**Emy:** Naturally.

**Liam:** Then she removed the batteries, put the same batteries back in, and pressed everything again.

**Emy:** The official technology repair process.

**Liam:** After that, she looked at me.

**Emy:** Why you?

**Liam:** I was standing nearest to the laptop.

**Emy:** So you were automatically promoted to technical support.

**Liam:** Exactly. Except I had no idea how to fix it.

**Emy:** What did you do?

**Liam:** I kept presenting.

**Emy:** Without slides?

**Liam:** Without slides.

**Emy:** You remembered the whole presentation?

**Liam:** Most of it.

**Emy:** "Most" sounds suspicious.

**Liam:** I had been so nervous the night before that I accidentally memorised everything.

**Emy:** What was the topic?

**Liam:** European revolutions. The Fronde, the revolutions of 1848, Hungary—

**Emy:** Wait. You remembered the dates?

**Liam:** Some of them.

**Emy:** Which ones?

**Liam:** I remembered 1648 for the Fronde. Obviously 1848. And Hungary was 1956.

**Emy:** That is annoyingly impressive.

**Liam:** Thank you.

**Emy:** I'm not complimenting you. I'm verifying the claim.

**Liam:** The fact police have approved one historical statement.

**Emy:** Temporarily.

**Liam:** So I carried on talking, and then I went to the whiteboard.

**Emy:** Why?

**Liam:** I needed a visual aid.

**Emy:** You drew one?

**Liam:** I drew Europe from memory.

**Emy:** Oh no.

**Liam:** It was recognisably Europe.

**Emy:** That is an extremely defensive description of a map.

**Liam:** Italy had the boot shape.

**Emy:** And the other countries?

**Liam:** They were nearby.

**Emy:** Nearby?

**Liam:** France was in the general France area.

**Emy:** Did you move Belgium?

**Liam:** Nobody complained.

**Emy:** Because nobody else knew where Belgium was.

**Liam:** The teacher looked impressed.

**Emy:** Or deeply concerned.

**Liam:** No, genuinely impressed. She let me finish the whole presentation.

**Emy:** What were your group members doing?

**Liam:** Standing behind me.

**Emy:** Helping?

**Liam:** One of them handed me a marker.

**Emy:** Critical contribution.

**Liam:** Another person nodded when I said something important.

**Emy:** So your group provided stationery and emotional support.

**Liam:** Exactly. But that experience genuinely taught me something.

**Emy:** That you can relocate several European countries without consequences?

**Liam:** That when the plan breaks, you keep going. If you act like you know what you're doing, people usually give you enough time to work it out.

**Emy:** That is actually useful.

**Liam:** Thank you.

**Emy:** Don't become unbearable.

**Liam:** Too late. What did school accidentally teach you besides threatening people with eye contact?

**Emy:** Storytelling.

**Liam:** How?

**Emy:** We had to write a report about a class trip to a museum.

**Liam:** Sounds normal.

**Emy:** I didn't go.

**Liam:** Why not?

**Emy:** I was sick.

**Liam:** So you didn't submit the report?

**Emy:** I submitted it.

**Liam:** About a trip you did not attend?

**Emy:** I asked two classmates what happened and reconstructed the experience.

**Liam:** You lied.

**Emy:** I researched.

**Liam:** You wrote in first person, didn't you?

**Emy:** Yes.

**Liam:** That is lying.

**Emy:** I included the exhibits, the lunch break, the broken water fountain—

**Liam:** Was there actually a broken water fountain?

**Emy:** Apparently.

**Liam:** "Apparently" is not helping.

**Emy:** I also described the security guard.

**Liam:** Did your classmates mention him?

**Emy:** Not exactly.

**Liam:** Emy.

**Emy:** I may have invented him.

**Liam:** You invented a museum employee?

**Emy:** I called him Dennis.

**Liam:** Why Dennis?

**Emy:** He felt like a Dennis.

**Liam:** You were not there!

**Emy:** The teacher gave me one of the highest marks in the class.

**Liam:** This is why trust in institutions is collapsing.

**Emy:** She wrote, "Excellent observational detail."

**Liam:** You observed nothing.

**Emy:** I observed other people describing it.

**Liam:** What did Dennis do?

**Emy:** Mostly stood near the entrance.

**Liam:** So even your fictional character had no development.

**Emy:** He was a supporting character.

**Liam:** Did anyone ask who Dennis was?

**Emy:** No. That was the lesson.

**Liam:** Which was?

**Emy:** You don't need twenty details to make a story feel real. You need three specific ones.

**Liam:** The broken fountain.

**Emy:** The squeaky museum floor.

**Liam:** Dennis.

**Emy:** Dennis carried the assignment.

**Liam:** Somewhere, a real museum employee has been replaced in history by a fictional man named Dennis.

**Emy:** Or maybe there was no guard.

**Liam:** That is somehow worse.

**Emy:** School teaches a lot of things that never appear on the syllabus.

**Liam:** Like how to choose a group-project partner based entirely on handwriting.

**Emy:** How to look busy when the teacher walks past.

**Liam:** How long the teacher has to be late before the class declares itself cancelled.

**Emy:** That rule was never real.

**Liam:** It was spiritually real.

**Emy:** How to hear your full name and immediately review every decision you made that week.

**Liam:** How to hide food inside a pencil case.

**Emy:** Why was food inside your pencil case?

**Liam:** Emergency biscuit.

**Emy:** That explains the ants.

**Liam:** We agreed not to discuss the ant incident.

**Emy:** I agreed to nothing.

**Liam:** So your useful school skill was inventing realistic details.

**Emy:** Storytelling.

**Liam:** Mine was continuing after the technology failed.

**Emy:** Improvisation.

**Liam:** And yours was controlling a room with one look.

**Emy:** Leadership.

**Liam:** You have renamed lying, intimidation, and panic very professionally today.

**Emy:** That may be the most useful school skill of all.

**Liam:** Making questionable decisions sound good?

**Emy:** Presentation skills.

**Liam:** Fair enough.

**Emy:** Chaos crew, tell us the strangest useful skill school accidentally taught you.

**Liam:** Maybe you learned negotiation from trading snacks.

**Emy:** Leadership from becoming class monitor.

**Liam:** Crisis management from losing your homework five minutes before class.

**Emy:** Or geography from watching someone draw Europe incorrectly on a whiteboard.

**Liam:** It was recognisable.

**Emy:** So was Dennis.

**Liam:** Dennis was imaginary.

**Emy:** And yet somehow more reliable than your projector.

**Liam:** That is where we're ending?

**Emy:** That is exactly where we're ending.

**Liam:** All right, chaos crew. Until next time, keep your own chalk.

**Emy:** And never trust a school projector.
